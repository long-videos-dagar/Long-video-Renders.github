Ezra Pike had been buried four times. And every time, he climbed out to complain about the service.

Dry Gulch, eighteen eighty. Ezra was the healthiest man in town, and the most dramatic. A cough? Funeral. A splinter? Funeral.

The first time, the coffin was too small. He kicked the lid off in the middle of the eulogy.

The second time, nobody cried. Ezra sat up and asked for names.

The third time, the undertaker charged him double. Ezra filed a complaint. From inside the grave.

By the fourth funeral, the town stopped coming. Even the priest just mailed a card.

So the undertaker made a decision. One perfect funeral. A velvet coffin. A brass band. Real tears. He paid for those.

Ezra lay there, listening, and for the first time in his life, he was happy. So happy, his heart simply stopped.

The band played. The undertaker wept. And the whole town finally showed up.

To laugh. Because everyone knew Ezra would climb out any second.

He didn't.

And nobody noticed for three days.
